# 6. Patience Tips — Slow Seas Stay Fun

*Ocean fishing is one of the few places in Eorzea that rewards relaxing. This page is about
keeping slow stretches enjoyable instead of anxious, both in-game and in life.*

**No tables on this page** — it's all words. Read it on the deck, if you like. Still
hand-maintained like every other page, and stamped with a verify date.

Last hand-checked: **2026-10-31**

## In the game

1. **Dead water is scenery time.** If the bites stop, look at the sky, the hull, the other
   fishers doing their idle animations. Almost every voyage gets slow minutes; they're part of
   the lighting budget, not a bug.
2. **Recast without guilt.** A long wait can simply be a bad pairing of bait to moment. Recast,
   try the other bait, move along. There's no score for "casts that deserved a fish."
3. **Set one small goal per voyage.** "Call out one boil." "Try the heavy bait once." "Read the
   fish-finder in every stretch." Small goals withstand weather; big goals wobble.
4. **Cheers are free.** Congratulate the fisher who lands the big one, and let others
   congratulate you. The scoreboard resets every trip; the warm chat habit is permanent value.
5. **Leave early is allowed.** If a real-life thing needs you, leave the voyage. The Endeavor
   sails again on its clock (see `01-voyage-windows.md`) and misses nobody, least of all you.

## In general (the part that travels)

1. **Slow progress is progress.** Fishing — digital or otherwise — pays out in accumulated
   evenings, not single dramatic hauls. Judge your collection in months, not windows.
2. **Match effort to energy.** Some trips you'll want perfect bait rotations; some trips you'll
   want to stare at water. Both are correct uses of the same boat.
3. **Spreadsheet dread is optional.** The moment a helper tool stops feeling like help, put it
   down and come back to the water. This manual was written so there's always somewhere warm to
   sit down again.
4. **Log tiny wins.** One line per voyage in your checklist ("caught the boot, called a boil,
   silver score accidentally") reads like a diary and works like a compliment you give yourself.
5. **Community over optimization.** Pick trip-mates and guides that make you feel good. The
   fish are imaginary; the friendliness isn't.

## When the sea is slow *and* you're impatient

Try this sequence, slowly: put down the bait, run the checklist from `voyage-checklist.md`, read
one page of the glossary in `glossary.md`, and cast again only when you actually want to. Nine
times out of ten, the impatience was about something else entirely, and the sea was just
patiently waiting for you to notice that.
