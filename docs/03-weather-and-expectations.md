# 3. Weather and Expectations — Explained Kindly

*Weather in ocean fishing is a mood-setter, not a gatekeeper. This page explains what the
conditions mean for your day on the water — and, just as importantly, what they don't mean.*

**Hand-maintained, not automated.** No sniffing, no scraping, no account data. Stamped by hand:

Last hand-checked: **2026-10-31**

## How weather shows up in ocean fishing

- Each stretch of the voyage has a sky. Rough skies, clear skies, storm fronts — they change what
  shows up on your fish-finder and, occasionally, what's worth pointing your line at.
- You don't need to pre-plan around weather the way land fishers sometimes do. The boat sails
  through its skies for you; your only job is to react warmly to what arrives.
- The fish-finder window (your little ocean radar) is honest: it lists the fish available in the
  current stretch. Re-read it when the weather turns. It's the single most under-used screen on
  the boat.

## Weather table (plain words, no math)

| Sky you'll see | What it feels like | What to do, kindly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear / sunny | relaxed, standard bites | steady bait rotation; enjoy the scenery |
| Cloudy | quieter water | good stretch to practice hook timing, no pressure |
| Rain / rainshowers | busier surface, good sound design | flush the small bites; points come easy |
| Rough seas / squall | dramatic, boat rocks | hold pole, keep casting; Spectral waters love a storm |
| Sharlayan-touched skies (glittering) | Spectral window is open | drop your best bait; don't leave the deck |

*(One stretch's taxonomy is another's typos — the row names above are descriptions, not exact
in-game strings. If your sky reads differently, trust the sky, log it, and update the table.)*

## Expectations, managed with love

Ocean fishing has a reputation problem caused by spreadsheets: people arrive expecting a points
engine and leave feeling they "wasted" the window. Reading this far means you're avoiding that.

1. **A bronze voyage is a finished voyage.** Points targets exist for achievement hunters and
   they are listed in [`05-achievement-hunts.md`](05-achievement-hunts.md) — but finishing the
   trip is itself the win.
2. **Spectral waters are a gift, not a schedule.** When they open, delight. When they don't,
   that's most voyages, and those voyages are still lovely.
3. **Nobody is counting your casts.** Fellow fishers who matter will never know how many bites
   you missed; they'll only know you called out a boil at the right time.
4. **The boat is rest.** Thirty minutes of sea, other fishers' chatter, the occasional large
   silhouette under the hull. Let it be that.

## If the weather feels bad for mood, not just fish

Some voyages genuinely feel slow. That's not you doing it wrong. Close the fish-finder, watch
the water, and treat the trip as a breather between other content. The sea will still be there
tomorrow, on its friendly fixed clock (see `01-voyage-windows.md`).
